# CodeAlpha_PhishingAwarenessTraining

An interactive cybersecurity awareness training presentation on recognizing, avoiding, and responding to phishing attacks.

> Task 2 — Cyber Security Internship, CodeAlpha

## 📌 Project Overview

A 14-slide training module designed to educate users on identifying phishing attempts across email, SMS, social media, and other channels — combining conceptual explanation with real-world examples and interactive quizzes to reinforce learning.

## 📚 Contents

1. **Introduction** — what phishing is and why it works
2. **Types of phishing** — credential phishing, malware phishing, and Business Email Compromise (BEC)
3. **Anatomy of a phishing email** — 6 concrete warning signs (sender spoofing, urgency, suspicious links/attachments, requests for secrets, odd wording)
4. **Spotting fake websites** — domain checks, HTTPS misconceptions, verification habits
5. **Social engineering tactics** — urgency, authority, fear, reward, curiosity, familiarity, reciprocity, scarcity
6. **Real-world case study** — the 2020 Twitter social engineering attack and its lessons
7. **Best practices** — MFA, independent verification, password hygiene, patching, fast reporting
8. **3 interactive quizzes** — realistic phishing scenarios with explained answers
9. **Incident response guide** — what to do if you click a phishing link
10. **Safety checklist & key takeaways**

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

- Understand what phishing is and why it's effective
- Recognize suspicious emails, links, attachments, and fake websites
- Identify common social engineering tactics attackers use
- Apply practical habits to protect accounts and data
- Practice decision-making through realistic, scenario-based quizzes

## 🛠 Format

- **PowerPoint (.pptx)** — `Phishing_Awareness_Training.pptx`
- Designed to be delivered as a live training session or a self-paced module

## 🎓 Key Takeaway

> Cybersecurity is not only a technology problem — it is a habit.

The core message reinforced throughout: **STOP → CHECK → VERIFY → REPORT.** A few seconds of caution can prevent a major security incident.
